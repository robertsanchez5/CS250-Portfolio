# CS 250 Portfolio – Software Development Lifecycle

## Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle at Southern New Hampshire University. The document reflects on my experience working with Agile and Scrum practices throughout the SNHU Travel project.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

Understanding user needs starts with identifying what the customer wants the software to accomplish. User stories help break those needs into smaller, manageable requirements that developers can work on. Throughout the SNHU Travel project, I learned how user stories provide direction for development and testing. They also help the team understand what features are important and how to determine whether those features meet the customer's expectations.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing programs, I believe it is important to start with clear requirements and break the work into smaller tasks. Agile development provides flexibility by allowing teams to make adjustments as requirements change. Through this course, I learned the value of Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives. In future development projects, I hope to use these practices to stay organized, communicate progress, and identify problems early instead of waiting until the end of a project.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for assigned work, and being willing to help others when needed. Software development requires collaboration between developers, testers, Product Owners, and other team members. Throughout this course, I learned that sharing information and addressing problems early can prevent delays and improve the final product. A successful Agile team depends on everyone working toward the same goal rather than focusing only on individual responsibilities.
